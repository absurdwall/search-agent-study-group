## Simple definition

Observable requirements that specify what must be true before a step or task counts as finished.

## Working definition

Completion criteria turn a vague instruction to finish into a checkable outcome. Chapter 4 recommends criteria that are both observable and sufficiently comprehensive to require the necessary work. They belong at the ends of steps and should make omissions visible instead of leaving success to a general impression.

## Why it matters

Clear finish lines resist premature completion and make review meaningful. They let a person or agent compare the result with the intended outcome and identify remaining work.

## Example

A chapter review is complete only after the full chapter has been inspected, every cited link has been checked, desktop and mobile layouts have been reviewed, and unresolved issues have been reported.

## Common confusion

A short task list or “make it good” is not a checkable definition of done. Passing one check does not prove all requirements were met, and natural-language criteria still need real execution and evidence.

## Study-group notes

Chapter 4’s writing review tightens each finish line. Its delivery example also checks repository standards and the agreed specification separately so success on one axis cannot hide failure on the other.
