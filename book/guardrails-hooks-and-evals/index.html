Study group guide · Chapter 5 Planned

Guardrails, Hooks, and Evals

A planned chapter on the evidence and intervention points needed to rely on agent runs repeatedly—not only make one run work once.

Provisional scope

The core topics are guardrails that constrain behavior, hooks that observe or intervene at meaningful lifecycle points, and evals that turn expected behavior into repeatable evidence.

Closely related topics to confirm include observability and tracing, human approval and escalation, failure recovery, and operational budgets such as timeouts, retry limits, and stopping conditions. The final chapter contract and source spine have not been selected.

Questions for the chapter contract

  • Which failures should be prevented, detected, contained, or escalated?
  • Where can hooks observe state without silently changing control flow?
  • What separates a structural check from a behavioral eval and an executed result?
  • How should traces, approvals, recovery paths, and budgets shape production confidence?
